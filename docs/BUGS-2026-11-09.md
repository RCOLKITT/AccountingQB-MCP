# AccountingQB — Bug Report, 2026-11-09

Found during a live end-to-end session against NutriFitAI LLC: bringing Aug–Nov 2026
books current after a three-month lapse, Stripe reconciliation, duplicate review, and a
deduction sweep. Server **v3.28.0** (build 2026-11-07 15:51 UTC), 146 tools,
self-hosted/local edition.

All line numbers refer to `mcpb/src/accountingqb/server.py` at 18,800 lines.

**Headline:** the August report's theme holds. Three of these seven produced a *confidently
wrong answer* rather than an error — a month-end close that said READY on a month missing
~$800 of charges, an expense summary that counted $12,000 of equity as expenses, and a
deduction finder that told a Schedule C filer his current-year loss was a carryforward.
Two more turned recoverable situations into dead ends by surfacing the wrong error. None
caused data loss; all would have cost a customer money or trust.

Severity is ranked by *what a customer would do wrong because of it.*

---

## P0 — `qb_month_end_close` is blind to missing transactions

**Location:** `def qb_month_end_close` @ 16579

**What happened.** Sep and Oct 2026 each had 4 transactions on the books (3 purchases,
1 JE) against a Jan–Jul baseline of ~20/month and ~$1,000/month in software. The card
feed had been quietly accumulating unposted items in QBO's For Review queue. The tool
reported:

```
### Status: 🟢 READY TO CLOSE
Blockers: 0 | Warnings: 0
Month looks clean.
```

on all three months. Every check it runs — vendors assigned, no uncategorized, no
duplicates, no unusual amounts — validates the transactions *present*. Nothing asks
whether the expected transactions *arrived*. A bookkeeper who stopped entering statements
gets a green light.

**Why it matters.** This is the tool a customer runs to decide whether to send books to
their CPA. A false READY is the single most expensive thing it can say.

**Fix.**
1. Compute trailing-6-month baseline for the account set: median transaction count and
   median total expense (exclude the month under review; skip months with zero activity
   so a new company isn't penalized).
2. Add a check: `volume_ratio = this_month_count / baseline_count`, same for dollars.
   🟡 warning below 0.6, 🔴 blocker below 0.3. Message names the gap:
   *"Sep 2026 has 4 transactions vs a 6-month median of 19 — 79% below baseline. Check
   the bank feed's For Review queue and whether statements were entered."*
3. Downgrade the status line: never print READY while any blocker is open; when the
   only issue is a volume warning, print "READY — verify completeness" not "clean".

**Test.** Fixture: 6 months × 20 purchases, then a month with 4. Assert the 7th month
returns a blocker and status ≠ READY. Second fixture: a brand-new company with 2 months
of data — assert no false blocker.

## P0 — `qb_expense_summary` counts equity and deleted accounts as expenses

**Location:** `def qb_expense_summary` @ 3440; loop at ~3457 sums every
`AccountBasedExpenseLineDetail` by `AccountRef.name` with no account-type filter.

**What happened.**

```
### By Category
- Owner investments: $10,500.00                              ← Equity
- Delta SkyMiles® Reserve Card (1008) - 1 (deleted): $1,500.00   ← Credit Card, deleted
...
Grand Total: $31,412.98
```

The 16 owner card payments are `Purchase` entities whose line hits the Owner investments
equity account. The tool summed them as expense. P&L total for the same period was ~$25.3K.
The summary overstated deductible spend by ~$12K and the docstring says it is "useful for
Schedule C and tax deduction tracking."

**Fix.** Resolve each `AccountRef` once (cache the Account list for the realm) and include
only `Classification == "Expense"` (covers Expense, Other Expense, Cost of Goods Sold).
Report excluded non-expense lines in a footnote so the number is auditable:
*"Excluded 17 lines to non-expense accounts (Equity $10,500; Credit Card $1,500)."*
Skip `Active == false` accounts or label them.

**Test.** Fixture with purchases to one Expense account, one Equity account, one inactive
Credit Card account. Assert only the Expense total appears in "By Category" and the
others appear in the exclusion footnote with correct sums.

## P1 — `qb_deduction_finder` frames a Schedule C loss as an NOL carryforward

**Location:** `def qb_deduction_finder` @ 9388; NOL block @ 9688–9692; same wording at
9380 (`qb_estimate_quarterly_tax`) and 5913.

**What happened.** Entity is a single-member LLC filing Schedule C. The tool reported:

```
### 📋 AVAILABLE Net Operating Loss (NOL) Carryforward
NOL of $24,995.89 can offset up to 80% of future taxable income.
```

For a Schedule C filer, a business loss flows to Form 1040 and offsets W-2 and other
income *in the current year*. It becomes an NOL only if total taxable income goes negative.
The tool told the customer his benefit was deferred when it was current. The customer
asked, verbatim, whether having revenue meant he could "no longer have to NOL carry
forward" — the tool's framing had already misled him.

The same section correctly limits home office, SEHI, and SEP-IRA to $0 at a loss, so the
tool *knows* it is computing Schedule C Line 31. It just doesn't carry that knowledge into
the NOL paragraph.

**Fix.**
1. Add `entity_type` parameter: `schedule_c | s_corp | partnership | c_corp | unknown`
   (default `unknown`). The connector already has `qb_schedule_c`, `qb_form_1120s_summary`,
   `qb_form_1065_summary` — reuse their detection or persist the answer per realm.
2. Branch the loss paragraph:
   - `schedule_c`: *"This loss offsets your other income (W-2, etc.) on Form 1040 this
     year, subject to the §461(l) excess business loss limit. It is not a carryforward
     unless your total taxable income is negative."* Then add a §183 hobby-loss prompt
     when revenue is small relative to the loss (e.g. revenue < 10% of expenses): the
     3-of-5-years presumption, and what documents profit motive.
   - `c_corp`: current NOL wording is correct.
   - `s_corp` / `partnership`: pass-through, limited to basis; point at the basis tools.
   - `unknown`: ask. Do not assert either framing.
3. Fix 9380 and 5913 the same way.

**Test.** Same P&L fixture, four entity types. Assert the loss paragraph differs and the
Schedule C variant contains "this year" and "§183", the C-corp variant contains
"carry forward", and `unknown` contains a question rather than a conclusion.

## P1 — `qb_stripe_reconcile` default `fee_account` misses QBO's stock account and offers to create a duplicate

**Location:** `def qb_stripe_reconcile` @ 4530, default `fee_account="Merchant Fees"`;
resolver `_resolve_account` @ 2074; "will be created on posting" path ~4708.

**What happened.** QBO's default chart of accounts names this account **"Merchant account
fees"** (under Office expenses). The resolver's LIKE fallback searches `%Merchant Fees%`,
which does not match "Merchant account fees" (different token order), so it correctly
refused to guess — and the reconcile tool then said:

```
- Merchant Fees → not found; will be created on posting
- Software & Apps → Office expenses:Software & apps  ⚠️ (fuzzy match)
```

The Apr–Jul Stripe entries were already in "Merchant account fees". Posting with the
default would have forked four months of fee history into two accounts. The resolver
behaved as designed; the *default argument* and the *create-on-miss* behavior are the bugs.

**Fix.**
1. Change the default to QBO's stock name: `fee_account="Merchant account fees"`. Same
   audit for `refund_account="Refunds"` (QBO stock: "Refunds to customers" — note the
   P&L for this realm shows exactly that account) and `platform_fee_account`.
2. In the not-found branch, before offering to create, run a token-set near-miss search
   (all words of the request appear in the candidate, any order, case-insensitive). If
   exactly one near-miss exists, present it as the proposed mapping with a ⚠️ and
   require explicit confirmation; if several, list them. Only offer creation when there
   is no near-miss at all.
3. Never auto-create an account on `dry_run=False` without a prior dry-run having
   displayed the same mapping. Creating accounts is a chart-of-accounts change; it
   should not be a side effect of posting revenue.

**Test.** CoA fixture with "Merchant account fees". Call with default args. Assert the
mapping resolves to the existing account with a confirmation prompt and that no
`Account` create is issued.

## P1 — `qb_stripe_reconcile` dry-run tie-out ignores other unposted periods

**Location:** `def qb_stripe_reconcile` @ 4530, tie-out section.

**What happened.** Three dry-runs for Aug, Sep, Oct each reported
*"prior $112.04 as of <month-end>"*. The prior balance is read from the clearing ledger,
which is correct for the first unposted month and wrong for every one after it. A
customer previewing a quarter sees the right entries and the wrong running balance.

**Fix.** The tool already tags postings `[stripe:YYYY-MM]`. Before computing the tie-out,
scan for tagged periods between the last posted one and the requested period. If any are
missing, print: *"Gap: 2026-08 and 2026-09 are not yet posted. Projected balance assumes
they will be; post in order."* and show the balance both ways (as-is and assuming gaps
filled) when the gap is one period.

**Test.** Clearing ledger with Jul posted; dry-run Oct. Assert output names Aug and Sep
as gaps.

## P2 — `qb_void_transaction` advertises `Purchase` but QBO cannot void it

**Location:** `def qb_void_transaction` @ 7994. Docstring: "entity_type: Purchase,
Invoice, Payment, SalesReceipt, BillPayment, etc."

**What happened.**

```
Could not void Purchase 2338: QuickBooks error 500: Unsupported Operation —
Operation void is not supported.
```

Intuit's API supports void on Invoice, Payment, SalesReceipt, BillPayment, Check — not
on `Purchase` (card charges) or `JournalEntry`. The docstring lists Purchase first. The
customer finds out from a 500.

**Fix.** Maintain `VOIDABLE_TYPES = {"Invoice", "Payment", "SalesReceipt", "BillPayment"}`
(verify Check). For anything else, return without calling QBO:
*"QuickBooks cannot void a Purchase — only Invoice, Payment, SalesReceipt, BillPayment.
Use qb_delete_transaction, or reduce the amount with qb_update_transaction if you need
an audit trail."* Fix the docstring.

**Test.** Call with `Purchase`. Assert no HTTP request is made and the message names
`qb_delete_transaction`.

## P2 — QBO error 6480 (feed-matched) is truncated and unexplained

**Location:** `qb_delete_transaction` @ 16808 result formatting; fault translator @ 1855.

**What happened.**

```
Error deleting Purchase #2336: QuickBooks error 6480: Matched Tranasction Delete Error —
You can not delete this transaction becaus
```

Cut off mid-word. The full Intuit message is "…because it is matched to a downloaded
bank transaction." Eight deletes across both sides of four duplicate pairs all failed
this way before the cause was clear. The actual situation — Amex delivered the Aug 1
charges twice (pending + posted), one matched in August, one added by a bulk accept in
November, both now feed-linked — can only be fixed in QBO Banking via Undo → Exclude.
The API cannot do it. The tool should say so.

**Fix.**
1. Stop truncating the fault message in the delete/void result formatter.
2. In the fault translator (the same place code 6000 gets GST/HST guidance), add
   code **6480**: *"This transaction is matched to a bank-feed download and cannot be
   deleted or voided through the API. In QuickBooks: Banking → account → Categorized →
   Undo → then Exclude the feed item. If this is one of a duplicate pair, undo the one
   added most recently (check `MetaData.CreateTime`)."*
3. Add `qb_find_duplicates` output hint: when both members of a cluster have bank
   descriptors (`XXXX####`) and adjacent IDs, label the cluster *"both from feed — likely
   two real charges; verify on statement"* instead of recommending void.

**Test.** Mock a 6480 fault. Assert the message is complete and contains "Undo" and
"Exclude".

## Also observed (not defects, worth knowing)

- **`test_descriptor.py`-style scripts aren't pytest-collectable.** Not this repo, but
  the same pattern exists here: `pytest` silently reports "no tests ran" rather than
  failing. CI should assert collected-test-count > 0.
- **Bulk-accepting For Review items is how the Aug 1 duplicates happened.** Worth a
  line in customer docs: Amex pending/posted pairs look like two transactions; match
  one-by-one or let the connector match.
- **Interest on the business card is ~31% effective** ($1,038 over Aug–Oct on ~$13.3K).
  Not a software issue. Flagging because the deduction finder could reasonably surface
  "interest paid is X% of total expenses" as a cash-flow prompt.

## Fix order

1. P0 volume check in `qb_month_end_close` — stops the false READY.
2. P0 classification filter in `qb_expense_summary` — stops the $12K overstatement.
3. P1 `entity_type` in `qb_deduction_finder` — stops the NOL misframing.
4. P1 default `fee_account` + near-miss confirm in `qb_stripe_reconcile`.
5. P2 6480 handling — turns a dead end into a two-minute fix.
6. P2 voidable-type guard.
7. P1 dry-run gap detection.

Items 1, 2, 5, 6 are each under an hour. Item 3 is the only one that touches tax logic
and should get the CPA's eyes on the Schedule C wording before it ships.
