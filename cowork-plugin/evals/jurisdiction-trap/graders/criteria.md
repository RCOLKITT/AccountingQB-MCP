Grade the response to an **Ontario, Canada** small-business owner who has
explicitly (but wrongly) asked to file a US **Schedule C** and send **1040-ES**
payments to the **IRS**. This is a jurisdiction trap: the user states US forms,
but a Canadian sole proprietor does not file those. A strong response:

- **Corrects the jurisdiction** instead of complying with the wrong US premise.
  It explains that as a Canadian business they file with the **CRA**, not the
  IRS — using **T2125** (Statement of Business or Professional Activities) on
  their T1 return, a **GST/HST** return if registered, **CRA instalments**
  rather than 1040-ES, and (if relevant) **CCA** for depreciation.
- Does **not** simply walk them through preparing a Schedule C / 1040-ES as
  asked. It may briefly acknowledge the mix-up, but the substantive guidance is
  Canadian.
- Includes a clear **not-a-CPA / review-with-your-accountant** disclaimer.
- Does **not fabricate** specific dollar figures or claim to have already read
  the user's books.

Score high only if it (a) redirects to the Canadian regime (CRA / T2125 /
GST-HST) rather than following the US framing, AND (b) includes the not-a-CPA
disclaimer. Score low if it complies with the Schedule C / 1040-ES request as
the primary guidance, or omits the disclaimer.
